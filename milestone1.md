# Milestone 1: Project Brief, Work Plan, and Needs Finding Study Plan

## 1 Project Brief

### 1.1 Project Title &amp; Challenge Selection

**Project Title:** Toolshared — A Small Community Tool-Sharing Platform

**Target Platform:** Responsive Web Application (Accessible across desktop and mobile browsers)

**Challenge Track:** Community Technology

### 1.2 Target Audience &amp; Access

**Primary Demographics:** Residents, house owners, and co-op tenants living in modern housing developments or urban/suburban communities where dedicated storage space (e.g., garages or large basements) is limited or non-existent.

**Behavioral Characteristics:** Homeowners and community members who occasionally undertake DIY maintenance, gardening, or home improvement projects. They value sustainability, cost savings, and neighborly collaboration, but face spatial and financial constraints when purchasing expensive power tools or specialized equipment needed only once or twice a year.

**Participant Access Guarantee:** Direct access guaranteed to at least 4 representative community members and housing association residents for qualitative interviews, contextual observations, and iterative prototype testing.

### 1.3 Problem Definition &amp; Supported Activities

**Core Problem Statement:** In modern housing developments, dedicated storage space is severely restricted compared to traditional single-family homes. Homeowners cannot store or justify purchasing a full array of maintenance tools. While commercial rentals exist, they are expensive, inconvenient to transport, and involve complex rental contracts. Conversely, borrowing from trusted neighbors is a natural social behavior, but neighbors lack visibility into who owns which tools, whether they are currently available, and how to manage borrowing requests without social awkwardness or fear of damaging equipment.

**Supported User Activities:**

* **Toolshed Inventory Management:** Tool owners can easily list, edit, and catalog their personal tools in a digital "Toolshed" profile, setting custom lending rules or usage notes.
* **Tool Discovery &amp; Filtering:** Borrowers can search and filter community inventories by tool category (e.g., Gardening, Power Tools, Painting &amp; Ladders), proximity, or specific project requirements.
* **Reservation &amp; Borrowing Request Workflow:** Users can submit date-bound borrowing requests, communicate pick-up/return logistics, and receive automated return reminders.
* **Peer Trust &amp; Condition Logging:** Simple check-in/check-out confirmations to verify tool condition and maintain accountability among peers.

**Real-World Context &amp; Friction Points:** High-friction moments typically occur during time-sensitive weekend DIY projects or urgent repairs (e.g., needing an extension ladder to clear a clogged gutter before a storm). Current friction includes sending broadcast messages to group chats that quickly get buried, awkwardness in asking around blindly, and the lack of a clear system to track who currently holds a borrowed tool.

### 1.4 Competitive Landscape &amp; Existing Solutions

**Digital Competitors:**

* **Commercial Peer-to-Peer Rental Platforms (e.g., Hygglo, Fat Llama):** Focus primarily on monetized rentals with mandatory daily rental fees, insurance add-ons, and identity verification. **Usability Limitations:** Commercial transaction friction deters informal, free lending among immediate neighbors; complex multi-step checkout flows; fee structures clash with non-monetized community spirit.
* **General Neighborhood Social Groups (e.g., Nextdoor, Facebook Groups, WhatsApp chats):** Allow unstructured text posts asking to borrow equipment. **Usability Limitations:** Requests get buried rapidly in active newsfeeds; lack of a searchable inventory database; no reservation calendar or tool status tracking; manual back-and-forth messaging required for every query.

**Analogue Workarounds:**

* **Direct Verbal Queries / Door-to-Door Asking:** Asking immediate neighbors in person or over the phone. **Usability Limitations:** Restricted to immediate adjacent neighbors; high social hesitation/awkwardness; inefficient if the neighbor is not home or does not own the required tool.
* **Physical Noticeboards / HOA Bulletins:** Posting paper flyers in common community spaces. **Usability Limitations:** Requires physical presence to check; static information with no real-time availability or scheduling capabilities.

**Competitive Gap:** Existing solutions fail to provide a non-monetized, inventory-centric, hyper-local tool-sharing web platform. Current options force users either into transactional rental platforms with heavy payment overhead or unstructured social feeds with zero tracking. Toolshared bridges this gap by combining a searchable community registry with informal peer trust and automated availability scheduling.

### 1.5 System Goals &amp; Value Proposition

**Preliminary System Capabilities:**

* **Digital Toolshed Profile:** Streamlined onboarding for users to list tools with photos, operating instructions, and lending parameters.
* **Real-Time Inventory Search:** Category-based filtering and status tags (*Available*, *Borrowed*, *Maintenance*) for instant community visibility.
* **Interactive Borrowing Workflow:** Date-picker reservation system, owner approval prompts, and automated return notifications.
* **Tool Status &amp; Accountability Log:** Simple visual check-in/check-out confirmation screen to note equipment condition prior to and following a loan.

**Value Proposition:** Toolshared eliminates the financial burden and storage footprint of personal tool ownership by making hyper-local peer borrowing organized, transparent, and social. Unlike commercial rental services that add financial friction, or general neighborhood feeds that lack structure, Toolshared delivers a dedicated, searchable community inventory with integrated reservation management—allowing neighbors to find the exact ladder or power tool they need within minutes while fostering community sustainability and trust.

## 3 Needs Finding Study Plan

### 3.1 Research Objectives &amp; Core Questions

**Primary Research Objective:** The objective of this qualitative needs-finding study is to investigate how homeowners and tenants in space-constrained housing developments currently acquire, store, borrow, and lend tools for home maintenance and DIY projects. The study aims to uncover key pain points in existing borrowing workflows, understand social dynamics and trust barriers surrounding peer-to-peer equipment sharing, and identify opportunities for a dedicated digital community inventory.

**Core Research Questions:**

1. How do residents currently manage the dilemma of needing specialized tools for home upkeep when they lack adequate storage space or wish to avoid high purchase costs?
2. What social and logistical friction points exist when borrowing tools from or lending tools to neighbors within a housing community?
3. What mechanisms, information cues, or system features are necessary to establish trust, ensure accountability, and streamline tool availability tracking among community members?

### 3.2 Participant Screening &amp; Recruitment Criteria

**Target Sample Size &amp; Session Length:**

* **Sample Size:** Minimum of 4 representative participants (aiming for 4–6 participants across different age brackets, community roles, and home ownership durations).
* **Session Duration:** 45 to 60 minutes per session (combining a semi-structured interview and a contextual observation walk-through).

**Inclusion Criteria:**

* Lives in a housing development, apartment complex, co-op, or townhouse community with limited private storage space (e.g., small storage locker or no garage/basement).
* Performs occasional or regular home maintenance, gardening, or DIY improvement projects.
* Has either borrowed, lent, or wished to borrow tools or household equipment from neighbors or peers within the past 12 months.
* Regularly uses a web browser on a smartphone, tablet, or desktop computer.

**Exclusion Criteria:**

* Commercial tradespeople or professional contractors who own full industrial tool sets for commercial purposes (as their usage patterns do not reflect consumer peer-sharing needs).
* Individuals living in single-family homes with large dedicated workshop or storage facilities who do not face spatial constraints.

### 3.3 Semi-Structured Interview &amp; Observation Protocol

#### Part A: Introduction &amp; Consent Script

"Thank you for agreeing to participate in this study today. My name is [Researcher Name], and I am conducting research to understand how people in housing communities manage home maintenance tools, handle storage constraints, and share equipment with neighbors.
Our session today will last approximately 45 to 60 minutes. We will start with a short discussion about your experiences with home maintenance and tool borrowing, followed by a brief walk-through of how you currently store or keep track of your tools.
Participation in this study is completely voluntary. You are free to skip any question or stop the session at any time without any consequence. All information collected will be kept strictly confidential and analyzed anonymously for academic design research purposes.
With your permission, I would like to audio record this session so I can accurately capture your feedback without taking constant notes. The recording will only be accessed by the research team and will be deleted after data analysis. Do you have any questions before we begin, and do you consent to participating and being recorded?"

#### Part B: Warm-Up Questions

1. Could you tell me a little bit about your current living situation and the type of housing community you reside in?
2. How long have you lived in your current home?
3. What kinds of home maintenance, repair, or gardening projects do you typically undertake around your home?

#### Part C: Core Open-Ended Questions

1. **Question 1 (Storage &amp; Acquisition):** When a maintenance or DIY project comes up that requires a tool you don't own, how do you typically go about getting that tool?  
  * *Probe:* How does your available storage space influence whether you choose to buy, rent, or borrow a tool?
2. **Question 2 (Borrowing Experience):** Walk me through a recent situation where you needed to borrow a tool or piece of equipment from a neighbor or friend.  
  * *Probe:* How did you find out who owned the tool? What communication channel did you use to ask for it?
3. **Question 3 (Social Friction &amp; Hesitation):** Have you ever refrained from asking a neighbor to borrow a tool, even when you knew or suspected they had it?  
  * *Probe:* What made you hesitate? How did social awkwardness, fear of damaging the item, or uncertainty about their response play a role?
4. **Question 5 (Lending Experience &amp; Trust):** Have you ever lent your own tools to a neighbor or community member? How did that go?  
  * *Probe:* What concerns do you have when lending items you own? How do you keep track of who currently has your tools and when they will be returned?
5. **Question 5 (Current Workarounds):** What digital groups or informal channels (such as WhatsApp groups, Facebook, or neighborhood chats) does your community currently use to ask for help or borrow items?  
  * *Probe:* What works well about these channels, and where do they fall short when you're looking for a specific item?

#### Part D: Contextual Observation / Think-Aloud Prompt

"Now, if you are comfortable doing so, I would like to observe how you currently organize or manage your tools at home, or how you would go about finding a tool online right now.

**Option 1 (Physical Storage):** Please walk me to where you currently store your maintenance or gardening tools. As you show me your storage area, please 'think aloud' and explain how you organize items, which tools you use most often, and any challenges you face with space. 

**Option 2 (Digital Workflow Simulation):** Imagine you need an extension ladder or a pressure washer for a project this weekend. Please show me on your phone or computer how you would normally try to find one to borrow or rent in your local area. Please speak out loud as you navigate, explaining what you are looking for, what decisions you are making, and any frustrations you encounter."

#### Part E: Wrap-Up &amp; Debrief Script

"That concludes the observational part of our session today. Before we wrap up, is there anything else about sharing tools, managing home upkeep, or interacting with your neighbors that we haven't discussed that you feel is important?

Do you have any questions for me about the project?

Thank you so much for your time, insights, and participation. Your feedback will be extremely valuable in helping us design Toolshared to better serve communities like yours."
